About

How I work with a stalled platform

I am an independent consultant. I help fintechs solve difficult problems with multi-tenant platforms on AWS and Kubernetes.

I have worked independently since December 1998. I began in embedded and high-integrity software, including the control software of a radiotherapy cancer treatment machine, and from 2018 I moved into AWS, Kubernetes and Terraform. Recent platform work includes NatWest Bank, HMRC and Pfizer. I also work with startups and scale-ups.

I do not advise from a distance. I have designed and built the parts of these platforms that clients struggle with, and they run in production. I then make the platform prove it works. I stay until it works, and I leave the client able to carry on without me. I like simple solutions.

I am based in London. I work with clients wherever they are, mostly remote, and I am happy to work on-site especially during discovery.

Case studies

Named organisations below are proof from regulated platforms. I work with fintechs on the same class of problem.

NatWest Bank · December 2024 to September 2025

Per-tenant egress for a multi-tenant Kubernetes platform

One set of outbound gateways per tenant, so a problem with one tenant stays with that tenant, and the SRE team learns about a failing gateway from an alert.

The platform passed its audit, and three tenants are using the egress solution in production.

Read the case study

NatWest Bank · December 2024 to September 2025

Observability you can actually trust

Grafana became a production service defined in Terraform, every metric and trace named its tenant, and the telemetry pipeline was made to prove it works.

30 engineers across three tenants were unblocked within one week. The checks found about 5% of metrics being silently lost.

Read the case study

Pfizer · September 2025 to September 2026

Getting rid of database passwords on a Kubernetes data platform

RDS IAM authentication, hidden behind a sidecar, so development teams did not have to rewrite their database code.

Without the passwordless database work, the platform would have failed an audit.

Writing

Notes for Heads of Platform and CTOs

  • 11 November 2026

    What should a fintech CTO learn about the GCP outage of 20th Oct 2026?

    The other availability zones in the region still degraded. High availability may need a multi-region decision, not only more zones.

  • 4 November 2026

    Is your multi-tenant platform becoming a business risk?

    At NatWest Bank, canaries found about 5% of metrics being silently lost, undetected for months.

  • 28 October 2026

    What does “multi-tenant” even mean?

    On a platform, a tenant is a team running applications on shared infrastructure, not a SaaS customer.

  • 21 October 2026

    When should a company build a multi-tenant platform rather than buy one?

    There is no off-the-shelf fully functional multi-tenant platform you can buy. A PaaS is a component, not the platform.

  • 14 October 2026

    A shared egress is how tenants become each other's incidents

    Shared capacity, shared configuration, shared identity and a shared failure domain.

  • 7 October 2026

    What should the platform team actually own?

    The goal is not to minimise ownership. It is to minimise the effort required to exercise it.

  • 30 September 2026

    Your dashboards are green, your platform is still broken

    Infrastructure metrics can look healthy while tenants still cannot build, deploy or reach what they depend on.

  • 23 September 2026

    Don't bolt observability onto your platform at the end

    Add a canary as each capability is built, so the platform has to prove it works from the start.

  • 16 September 2026

    What a CTO should know before choosing Kubernetes

    You might be able to fulfil all your platform requirements with a cheap, simple solution.
